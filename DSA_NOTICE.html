1. About This Notice

This notice provides information required by the EU Digital Services Act (Regulation (EU) 2022/2065) for users of the kicknap Platform. It applies to all Members (Hosts and Guests) and visitors in the European Economic Area (EEA), Switzerland, and the United Kingdom.

kicknap is an online marketplace within the meaning of Article 3(i) of the DSA, providing intermediary services that connect Hosts and Guests for hourly space rentals.

3. Description of the Service

kicknap is an online platform that:

  • Allows Hosts to list spaces (desks, meeting rooms, studios, napping spaces) available for hourly rental
  • Allows Guests to search for, book, and pay for hourly access to listed spaces
  • Facilitates payments between Guests and Hosts through Stripe Connect
  • Provides communication tools between Hosts and Guests
  • Provides a Resolution Center for dispute handling

4. Content Moderation (Articles 14-17 DSA)

4.1 Notice and Action Mechanism (Article 16)

Any person or entity can notify kicknap of allegedly illegal Content on the Platform by contacting:

Email: content@kicknap.com

Subject Line: DSA Notice — Illegal Content Report

A valid notice must include:

  1. A statement of good faith belief that the Content is illegal
  2. The specific location (URL) of the Content
  3. A description of why the Content is allegedly illegal
  4. The reporter's name and contact information
  5. A statement confirming the accuracy of the report

4.2 Our Response (Article 16(4))

Upon receiving a valid notice, kicknap will:

  1. Acknowledge receipt of the notice without undue delay
  2. Swiftly review the reported Content
  3. Take a reasoned decision regarding the Content (removal, restriction, or no action)
  4. Notify the reporter and the affected Member of our decision with a statement of reasons
  5. Allow the affected Member to use internal complaint mechanisms if they disagree

4.3 Automated Moderation (Article 17)

kicknap may use automated tools to detect and moderate Content that violates our Terms. When automated means result in Content being removed or restricted:

  • The affected Member will be notified
  • The notification will explain that automated means were used
  • The Member may request human review of the decision

5. Internal Complaint Handling (Article 20)

If you are affected by a content moderation decision and wish to contest it, you may file an internal complaint by:

  1. Emailing complaints@kicknap.com with your complaint
  2. Providing the Content ID or reference number from the original decision
  3. Explaining why you believe the decision was incorrect

kicknap will review your complaint and provide a decision within a reasonable time. For EEA users, this decision will be made by a person not involved in the original decision, where technically feasible.

6. Out-of-Court Dispute Settlement (Article 21)

If you are not satisfied with the outcome of our internal complaint handling, you may refer the matter to a certified out-of-court dispute settlement body designated by the relevant Digital Services Coordinator.

A list of designated bodies is available at: European Commission — DSA designated bodies

7. Trusted Flaggers (Article 22)

kicknap cooperates with trusted flaggers as designated by the relevant Digital Services Coordinator. Notices from trusted flaggers receive priority processing.

kicknap does not currently have formal agreements with trusted flaggers. This will be updated as trusted flagger designations become available in the Netherlands.

8. Protection Against Abusive Notices (Article 22(4))

kicknap protects Members against clearly unfounded or abusive notices. If kicknap determines that a notice is clearly unfounded or abusive, we may:

  • Notify the reporter and explain why the notice was rejected
  • Restrict the reporter from submitting further notices
  • Report the matter to the relevant Digital Services Coordinator if appropriate

9. Transparency Reporting (Article 15)

As a very small provider, kicknap will publish transparency reports when required by the DSA (starting from February 2026 for providers meeting the thresholds). kicknap commits to voluntarily publishing transparency reports earlier, including:

  • The number of notices received and actions taken
  • The number of internal complaints received and outcomes
  • The number of automated moderation decisions and human review requests

10. EU Short-Term Rental Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2024/1028)

In compliance with Regulation (EU) 2024/1028 on data collection and sharing in the short-term rental sector, kicknap facilitates compliance with the following requirements:

10.1 Host Registration Numbers

Hosts must provide a valid unique identification number (or registration number) issued by the relevant local or national authority in their Listing where such a registration system exists. kicknap may verify the format of registration numbers but does not verify their validity with the issuing authority. Hosts are solely responsible for obtaining and maintaining valid registration numbers where required by local law.

10.2 Data Sharing with Authorities

kicknap will share data with competent local and national authorities as required by Regulation (EU) 2024/1028, including information necessary for the enforcement of applicable short-term rental rules. This may include host identification, property identification, booking dates, and number of nights booked. Data sharing will be conducted in compliance with the GDPR.

10.3 Listing Requirements

Hosts must ensure their Listing includes: (a) their registration number (where required by local law), (b) the contact details of the Host, (c) the type of accommodation, and (d) the applicable local rules governing short-term rentals. kicknap is not responsible for the accuracy of registration numbers provided by Hosts.
